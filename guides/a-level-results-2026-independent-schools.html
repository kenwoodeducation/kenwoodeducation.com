A-Level Results 2026: Independent Schools In and Around North London

How common top A-level grades are at eight selective independent schools North London families consider, and what that means for university applications.

The short version

  • At these schools, between 55% and 87% of A-level grades were A* or A. Nationally it was 28.3%.
  • The top four schools are within a few points of each other, so the gaps are small.
  • When top grades are this common, admissions tests, interviews and personal statements matter more.

How to read it

  • Small gaps aren’t meaningful. Highgate at 87% and South Hampstead at 86% are effectively level. Each sixth form is small, so a few grades can move the percentages.
  • These are grades, not students. They don’t show how many students got straight A*s.
  • Selectivity drives much of the variation. Schools with competitive entry draw a higher-scoring intake, so their results reflect that as well as the teaching. A school lower in the table is still well above the national average.

What it means for university applications

When most grades at a school are A* or A, strong predicted grades are the norm. For competitive courses, that puts more weight on the rest of the application: admissions tests, interviews where they apply, the personal statement, and depth in the subject beyond the syllabus. That is often where extra preparation makes the biggest difference.

What to do with this

  • Ask the school how your child’s predicted grades compare with their year group, not just the national average.
  • For Oxford, Cambridge or other competitive courses, start preparing for tests and interviews early.
  • If a subject is lagging, deal with it early in Year 12 rather than after the mocks.
