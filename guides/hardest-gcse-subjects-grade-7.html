Which GCSE Subjects Are Hardest to Get a Grade 7 In?

National 2026 results data on how common a grade 7 actually is in each subject, why Maths and English aren't the easy wins people assume, and what that means for where to focus support.

The numbers

Figures below are from the Joint Council for Qualifications (JCQ), the body representing all the UK's exam boards, and reflect its own 2026 national results, covering all UK entries across the most widely taken GCSE subjects, ordered by the percentage of entries awarded a grade 7 or above.

For context, the national average across all GCSE subjects sits at 22.0%. Combined Science, English Language and Mathematics, the three single biggest GCSEs by entry numbers in the country, all sit below that average. Chemistry, Physics and Biology sit well above it.

Why this isn't a straight difficulty ranking

It's tempting to read this table as "Chemistry is easier than Maths," but that's not quite what it shows. Maths, English Language and Combined Science are sat by almost every GCSE student in the country, including plenty of students for whom a grade 7 was never a realistic target. That drags the subject's overall 7+ rate down, not because the exam is necessarily harder, but because the cohort sitting it is unfiltered.

Chemistry, Physics and Biology work differently. Many schools only offer the three separate sciences, rather than Combined Science, to their stronger students, while the rest of the year group takes Combined Science instead. That pre-selection means the cohort sitting Chemistry is already skewed toward higher attainers, before a single exam is marked. The subject's high grade-7 rate partly reflects who's in the room, not just what's on the paper.

None of this means Maths or English are "the hard subjects" in some absolute sense. It means that, for an individual student, closing the gap to a 7 in Maths or English Language is statistically a steeper climb than closing the same gap in Chemistry or Physics, because fewer entries clear that bar nationally in the first place.

What this means for where to focus support

If a child is sitting on a 6 and the target is a 7, the subject matters. A borderline 6/7 in Maths or English Language is, nationally, a harder gap to close than the same borderline in one of the separate sciences, simply because so many fewer students clear it. That's not a reason to assume the sciences need less attention, but it is a reason to take a Maths or English borderline seriously rather than treating it as the "easier" subject to leave until later.

It's also worth remembering that a school's own grade distribution can look very different from the national one, particularly at a selective school. The right comparison for your child isn't just this national table, it's where they sit within their own year group.

What to do with this

  • If a child is borderline in both a science and Maths or English, the national data suggests the language/humanities-adjacent or numeracy gap is statistically harder to close, and may be worth prioritising.
  • Ask the school how your child's grade compares within their own cohort, not just against the national average. A 6 can mean very different things depending on who else is in the room.

Frequently asked questions

Which GCSE subject is hardest to get a grade 7 in?
Among the most widely taken GCSE subjects, Combined Science (Double Award) has the lowest grade 7+ rate nationally at 9.4%, followed by English Language at 15.3% and Mathematics at 17.0%, based on 2026 JCQ results.

Is Chemistry easier than Maths at GCSE?
Not necessarily. Chemistry's grade 7+ rate is far higher than Maths' (47.1% versus 17.0% in 2026), but that mostly reflects who sits each exam, not how hard it is. Chemistry is usually taken by a smaller, more academically selective group, while Maths is sat by almost every student in the country, including many for whom a 7 was never a realistic target.

What is the national average GCSE grade 7 rate in 2026?
22.0% of all GCSE entries across the UK were awarded a grade 7 or above in 2026, according to JCQ's national results data.

Which GCSE subject has the highest grade 7 rate?
Among the most widely taken GCSE subjects in 2026, Chemistry had the highest grade 7+ rate at 47.1%, followed closely by Physics at 46.1% and Biology at 43.6%.
