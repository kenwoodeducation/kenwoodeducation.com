What Do GCSE Grades Actually Mean?

Since 2017, GCSEs in England have used a 9-1 numeric scale instead of the old A*-G letters. Here's a clear translation, and the one distinction most explainers miss.

The translation

New gradeOld grade (approx.)
9Above A*
8High A*/A
7A
6High B
5Strong pass (high C/low B)
4Standard pass (C)
3D
2E
1F/G

The distinction most people miss

A grade 4 and a grade 5 are both commonly described as a "pass," but they aren't treated the same in practice. Grade 4 is the government's standard pass, the minimum most institutions require. Grade 5 is the "strong pass," and it's typically the benchmark sixth forms and colleges are actually looking for.

That gap matters more than the simpler pass/fail framing suggests, particularly when it comes to planning next steps and subject choices.

Worth knowing

If a subject is showing a 3 or 4, it's worth understanding exactly where that sits and what it means for progression, rather than treating any pass as equally secure.
