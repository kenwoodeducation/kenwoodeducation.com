Safeguarding Policy

Our commitment to safeguarding and promoting the welfare of every child and young person we work with, and what's expected of every Kenwood tutor.

Our Commitment

Kenwood Education is committed to safeguarding and promoting the welfare of all children and young people we work with. Every tutor on our roster is vetted thoroughly before being introduced to any family, and we take any safeguarding concern seriously and act on it promptly.

Designated Safeguarding Lead

Ondi Tongchaitanawutti, Director of Kenwood Education, is our Designated Safeguarding Lead. Any safeguarding concern — from a tutor, a family, or anyone else — should be reported to Ondi directly and without delay.

Safe Recruitment

Every tutor undergoes our full vetting process before being placed with any family, including:

  • A structured interview assessing experience, approach, and suitability to work with children
  • Two references, at least one from someone who has directly observed the tutor teaching, tutoring, or working with children or young people
  • An enhanced DBS check, ideally registered on the Update Service, re-verified annually
  • Identity verification, conducted as part of the DBS application process

No tutor is introduced to a family until every stage of this process is complete.

Working in Family Homes

Given the nature of in-person, in-home tutoring, we ask that:

  • A responsible adult is present in the property throughout any session involving a student under 18
  • Tutors maintain clear, professional boundaries at all times, and avoid any private or unsupervised contact with a student outside agreed sessions
  • Tutors do not contact students directly via personal social media or personal messaging outside the context of agreed tutoring
  • Tutors do not accept gifts from students or families beyond a nominal value (e.g. over £50)

Code of Conduct for Tutors

Tutors are expected to:

  • Behave professionally and appropriately at all times when working with children and young people
  • Treat every student with respect, patience, and encouragement
  • Avoid any behaviour that could be perceived as favouritism, inappropriate familiarity, or a breach of professional boundaries
  • Never be alone with a student in circumstances that could not be explained or justified
  • Raise any concern about a student’s welfare immediately, however small it may seem

Recognising a Concern

A safeguarding concern might include (but isn’t limited to):

  • A disclosure made by a student, directly or indirectly
  • Signs of physical, emotional, or other harm
  • Behaviour that suggests a student is at risk, whether at home, at school, or elsewhere
  • Concern about the conduct of another adult in the household

Tutors do not need to be certain something is wrong to raise a concern — if in doubt, raise it.

What to Do

If a child is in immediate danger, call 999.

Otherwise, contact Ondi (Designated Safeguarding Lead) immediately, by phone or in person where possible. Ondi will:

  • Listen to and record the concern accurately
  • Decide, with appropriate advice where needed, what action to take
  • Contact the NSPCC Helpline (0808 800 5000) for expert guidance where useful
  • Escalate to children’s social care or the police where appropriate
  • Keep a written record of the concern and any action taken

Tutors should never investigate a concern themselves, or promise a child confidentiality — any concern shared with a tutor may need to be passed on.

Confidentiality

Information shared in relation to a safeguarding concern is shared only with those who need to know, in line with our data protection obligations — but safeguarding a child’s welfare always takes priority over confidentiality where the two are in tension.

Record-Keeping

Safeguarding concerns and actions taken are recorded and retained securely, separately from general tutor/family records, for as long as necessary to safeguard the child and meet any legal obligation.

Review

This policy is reviewed periodically and updated as needed to reflect current best practice and any change in our operating model.
